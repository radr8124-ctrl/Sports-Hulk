Creative guardrail: elevate the existing design without losing its retro broadcast charm; animation is optional, extremely quick, and nonblocking. Keep user choice and secure sign-in existing integration unchanged during current Survivor review.

## Non-cheesy design acceptance — owner requirement (October 10)
**Critical: must feel expensive, confident, and mature, never cheesy.** The "retro-modern sports" character comes from typography, discipline, precision, and the existing dashboard's navy/cream/aqua/coral palette—not nostalgic sports clip art, casino marketing, video-game splash screens, fake scoreboards, or elaborate arena graphics. Avoid giant stadium spotlights, smoke, fireworks, mascot imagery, spinning logos, 3D chrome effects, artificial winning streaks, neon overload, inspirational slogans, and loud "ENTER THE ARENA" style copy. Treat the earlier sample slogan **YOUR FRONT ROW TO THE GAME** as a discarded option, not the approved headline.

Prefer a minimal SPORTS ZENITH wordmark and brief positioning text (e.g. "Sports intelligence, in focus."), refined editorial type, one quiet hero arrangement that carries through to the real dashboard, a tasteful single accent line or small real-data-inspired grid, and clear **Enter** / **Sign In** controls. Motion may be one subtle, fast reveal, with no autoplay audio and an instant skip or reduced-motion experience. No unnecessary mandatory splash page or sign-in wall on public routes. The decisive owner acceptance test is: side-by-side with the actual homepage, the welcome feels like one seamless premium product, not a different aesthetic or a flashy template. First prototype must favor restrained sophistication over spectacle; get owner approval before deployment.

## First-impression conversion requirement — owner emphasis
**This is the very first experience new visitors see. The objective is to earn their attention immediately and make them WANT to enter Sports Zenith.** Being merely elegant is not enough: the screen must create genuine interest within the first three seconds while remaining mature and on-brand. A visitor should instantly understand (1) **Sports Zenith** is the product, (2) it offers **sports intelligence beyond ordinary scores**, and (3) exactly **where to tap to enter**. This is a top-level final-launch acceptance requirement, not an optional ornament.

First-screen composition: powerful, restrained SPORTS ZENITH wordmark; one specific, compelling, short editorial statement about *seeing the game more clearly* (draft example: **BEYOND THE SCORE.**, subject to owner review); a subtle sport-data-inspired graphical moment matching the real dashboard; a large, unmistakable primary **Explore Sports Zenith** action for first-time guests; a distinct **Sign In** action for returning members. Do not ask an unknown visitor to create an account merely to see the public product. Place the primary CTA and the sign-in action on the **first iPhone viewport without scrolling** (320px–430px tested). After entering, the real site's visual language should feel like an uninterrupted continuation of the same product.

Animation can deliver one understated visual hook (e.g., elegant broadcast-line reveal and a quiet data-card transition) but never become a time-wasting intro: allow immediate interaction, no forced waiting, no flashing LEDs, no 3D, no sound, and full reduced-motion support. Use the existing passwordless email-code flow; never rebuild authentication for this design. Honor saved sign-in and deep links, and avoid showing the screen repeatedly to a returning member. Consider a split-screen desktop treatment only if mobile remains the strongest design. The emotional impression should be **intrigue + confidence + credibility**, not nostalgia, gimmicks, or betting-site hype.

**Acceptance checklist before deployment:** side-by-side preview versus the existing homepage at 320/390/430/768/1280px; subjectively approved as visually cohesive and attention-grabbing without cheese; above-the-fold action visibility; first paint fast on iPhone/mobile networks; real Enter path; real existing email-code sign-in path; guest access to public pages; deep-link preservation; return-member bypass; keyboard and screen-reader controls; reduced motion; zero fabricated source numbers. Do not deploy until the owner approves the actual look and real navigation is verified.

## Product safeguards
- **Build after** the pending Survivor owner review and core source-proof work; do not interrupt current production sign-in during Week 5 review.
- **Reuse** the existing secure email one-time-code authentication and member-linked Survivor/DFS/fantasy storage. No separate login database, no password storage and no hidden account-claim bypass.
- Support returning member access, account creation using the same verified email code, and access to public previews where appropriate. Do not make an arbitrary gate that breaks public Scores/News/SEO.
- Preserve direct links to `/` and `#survivor` and the existing navigation/entry state; signed-in visitors may go straight into the site rather than being forced through an animation every time.
- Respect reduced-motion/accessibility and real iPhone performance. Fast first paint, compressed media, clear keyboard focus, readable contrast, no auto-playing audio, and a quick skip/enter path. Do not make the site slower to obtain a flashy video.
- Any displayed scores, market lines, injury alerts or performance stats must come from currently verified data; do not put fake live odds, wins or model accuracy on the opening page.
- Prototype and review with owner FIRST. Verify mobile and desktop, sign-in email code, deep-link return, guest route, private-account access, accessibility and fallback before production release.
